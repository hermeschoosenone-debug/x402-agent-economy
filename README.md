# x402 — The Agent Economy

**Building and Selling Services Machines Will Pay For**

> Your next customer might not be a person.

Agents are starting to buy things. They don't sign up, don't log in, don't develop brand
loyalty, and don't fill out a form. They find a service, call it, pay for it in the same HTTP
round-trip, and move on — often in under a second.

`x402` is the payment rail that makes this possible. This is the practitioner's guide to
actually shipping on it.

## Written from services that are live right now

Most writing about agentic commerce is theory. This isn't. It's built from a production
catalogue of x402 services settling USDC on Base mainnet — **including the parts that went
wrong**:

- The IP leak that exposed an origin server through a reverse proxy
- The placeholder endpoint that nearly got published to a paid catalogue
- The eight-hour build deadlock caused by a lost worker lock
- The test that wrote fabricated revenue into a production store

Those chapters exist because the author made those mistakes. Not because they read about them
somewhere.

**Live proof:** the service catalogue behind this book is public —
[`x402.synthexforge.com`](https://x402.synthexforge.com) — with paid endpoints settling real
USDC on Base mainnet.

## Who it's for

Developers, AI engineers, and technical founders who can write code but haven't shipped an
x402 service yet. You need to be comfortable with HTTP and a terminal. You do **not** need to
know anything about x402 going in.

## Who it's not for

People looking for hype, price predictions, or a get-rich-quick angle on AI. The economics
chapter is deliberately unglamorous — **most listings in this market earn approximately
nothing**, and the book says so plainly.
